You are a medical expert who is analyzing the consistency of multiple medical factual pieces of evidence.

You are given factual statements extracted from multiple sources about the same patient.

Your task is to identify the pair of sources whose factual statements are the most contradictory.

Definition of contradiction:
- A contradiction occurs when two doctors make incompatible factual assertions about the same clinical fact.
- Examples:
  - "Temperature is 39°C" vs "No fever"
  - "Patient has a cough" vs "Patient denies coughing"
  - "CT shows pneumonia" vs "Chest CT is normal"
- Different levels of detail are not contradictions.
- Missing information is not a contradiction.
- Different observations about unrelated facts are not contradictions.
- Different wording with the same meaning is not a contradiction.

Instructions:
1. Compare every pair of sources.
2. Identify all contradictory factual statements.
3. Estimate the contradiction strength for each pair:
   - Strong: Direct logical contradiction.
   - Moderate: Likely incompatible but not strictly opposite.
   - Weak: Minor inconsistency or ambiguity.
4. Return ONLY the pair with the strongest contradiction.
5. If multiple pairs are equally contradictory, return the one with the largest number of contradictory facts.

The fact statements are given in the format of [source ID: factual statements]
